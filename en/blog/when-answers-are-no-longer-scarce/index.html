I used to think everyone would be satisfied one day

I used to imagine a future where everyone would be satisfied. My dad said people are greedy, and there is only one beach. Later I came to think he had a point: people want things once they have seen something better, so the day of enough never comes. Life probably won't change dramatically, but it will be a little freer.

I used to imagine the future as a world where everyone would be satisfied.

Once, on a walk by the river with my dad, I told him about this. There would be a huge system, with intelligence in software and robots doing physical work, and everyone could get support from it: however big your heart, that much support. If you wanted to make a game, agents would write the code, draw the art and make the music. If someone in your family got sick, agents would read the papers and look for treatments. If you wanted to live in a world with two moons, where people live on huge floating islands, you would describe it and it would build that world for you.

I talked about a lot of things like this that day, and I was happy talking about them.

There is only one beach

When I finished, my dad said people are greedy. However much there is, people will still compare, compete and envy. He gave an example: there is only one beach, and not everyone can be on it.

My answer at the time was that we could terraform other planets and make more beaches. I still think so. Next to the universe the Earth is small, and with very strong AI and automated factories this will slowly become possible.

But part of what he said was right. However big your heart, that much support can't be true for everyone. If I suddenly decided I wanted to explore the universe, something that costs that much can't be done by everyone who feels like it.

He also said that day that something felt missing from what I described, but he couldn't say what. I thought about that for a long time afterwards.

The emperor couldn't get KFC

An ancient emperor wouldn't complain that his food was bad. Even if he got tired of it, he couldn't imagine KFC, let alone eat it. If someone from ancient times came to live on today's basic welfare allowance, they might live better than the emperor did. But to me today, living on that allowance isn't acceptable: I couldn't even have KFC and Burger King often.

The only wants we are born with are probably to survive and to have children. Every other want comes from first seeing something better and realising things could change. The more you understand, the more better things you see, and the more you want. There is no ceiling. So however rich the world becomes, there won't be a day when it's enough, and the world where everyone is satisfied that I used to imagine probably won't happen.

I think this is what my dad meant by something missing.

No ceiling is a good thing

Still, I think having no ceiling is good. Wanting more means people will keep going forward, and that is where progress comes from. When I'm chasing something, I always feel it isn't good enough yet, but while I'm chasing it I'm absorbed in it, and happy.

Climbing up, or pulling others down

It has an ugly side too.

When I post on X, sometimes I'm happy with what I wrote and it gets no interaction at all, while someone else posts something baffling and gets a huge number of views. Then I feel it's unfair, and I feel two things at once: I want that too, and I'm angry, even a bit like I want to destroy something. When we were kids, adults taught us to tell admiration apart from envy, probably because people have both.

When someone else has something you don't, there are two ways to close the gap: climb up yourself, or pull them down. Climbing up takes ability and time. Pulling someone down is usually much easier.

My dad has also said that the tighter life is somewhere, the more people scheme against each other. I think this is why: when your own ability to climb is limited, the path of pulling others down looks very close.

A little freer

Thinking it through like this, I don't expect life to change dramatically. People will still want more, and still compare and compete.

I used to think at least one thing was certain: feeding ourselves can be left entirely to AI and machines, and people won't have to work just to survive. Now that seems a bit idealistic. By ancient standards, surviving today isn't hard, yet I feel more than nine in ten people who work are still pushed by the pressure to get by, and that pressure includes staying alive as well as starting a family and raising children. There has never been a line between living and living better.

So now all I can say is what I hope: that the economic value AI creates on its own can be used to support people, and that this support gets better as AI does. This year everyone can afford KFC, next year everyone can afford a buffet. Then at least nobody has to worry about their life getting worse. Who would share it out, and whether they would be willing to, I haven't figured out.

With that kind of support, the pain of comparing would still be there, but there would be much less worry about making a living, and people could choose more freely what to put themselves into. People have always had very limited freedom, in ancient times and now, and it won't be much bigger in the future. We will just be able to see more, and find it easier to change the things around us when we want to.

So the future I imagine now is one where everyone gets a limited share of resources and uses it to build a world they like. How big that share is depends on what AI and machines can do by then, and on how things are shared out, but what you build inside it is up to you. The two moons and floating islands I told my dad about that day could be built there.
