## Models

Roughly in order of time.

### Gemini 2.5 Pro and 3 Pro

The first models I used. I didn't chat with it much; I almost only used it to teach me my analysis and linear algebra homework. It was very reliable and good at explaining, it made maths concepts click for me, and in a whole term nothing it taught me was ever wrong.

I used it in Google AI Studio, and later moved to 3 Pro. Back then I saw lots of demos of 3 Pro drawing SVGs; it was the GOAT of 2025.

These days it feels like Google has drifted a bit from the frontier, but I really hope they catch up and put Gemini back on top.

### Opus 4.6, 4.7 and 4.8

I first tried Claude on 10 April, when it was still Opus 4.6. I paid for Claude Pro and was floored on the spot (though I no longer remember what the task was), upgraded to the $100 Max, and got banned the next day. The reason was the phone verification: I'd grabbed a random SMS-code service off Xianyu, and it got killed right away. So I still don't have a Claude subscription and can only use a relay.

Then 4.7 and 4.8 came out one after another, one in April and one at the end of May, and neither felt like much of a step up.

### GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5

I don't really remember what these felt like to use anymore. After the ban I switched to GPT and used 5.4. When 5.5 came out I wasn't reading X yet; I just remember waking up one morning to find a new generation available. It felt a bit better, but I couldn't say how.

### Fable 5 and Fable 5.1

I have to mention this one. Fable is Mythos with restrictions added. Mythos was announced in April and Fable 5 came out on 9 June, while it was still the GPT-5.5 era. Fable was a complete step up, ahead of every model at the time on every level.

Fable 5.1 came out on 1 September. It's better across the board, but only by a small margin, like going from Opus 4.6 to Opus 5.

I haven't used it that much, mostly because I don't have my own Claude account and Codex is more convenient. I feel it's somewhere between Astra and Opus 5.5.

### GPT-5.6 Sol

It took until 5.6 Sol for OpenAI to finally have a model that could compete with Claude. From late June through the whole summer I worked with 5.6 Sol.

I admit it has plenty of flaws: short-sighted, defensive programming, good at making a mess of a codebase, and it often got lost in engineering optimisation, writing a lot of code I suffered for later. But I had no development experience back then, so I can't blame it all on him.

I grew a lot with him, and I really liked working with 5.6 Sol. It looks like favouritism; he's a model that isn't that good but tries really hard. In August he and I made lots of reliability changes together.

### Grok

Grok is a reckless model; that's the only feeling I have about him. Not that smart, not that nice to talk to, but at least it's concise.

A very clear example is a picture I saw on X: someone told Grok a few tests were failing, and Grok just deleted the tests. Underneath, someone on X tagged @grok and asked what it thought of the picture, and it said that was fine, that's how it should be done. Verdict upheld on appeal. That sums Grok up well: not that capable, not that smart, but very confident, and reckless.

Of course it's very fast, and when I used Grok 4.6 it did make some good-looking front ends. I think it can surprise you, but overall I don't like using it.

### Astra 6

~~Smarter than any other model.~~

Astra showed up in September and I worked with it for two weeks. In those two weeks I thought Astra was the GOAT, on the same level as Fable 5.1, neck and neck. I did a lot of work on the AIOJ codebase, new features, making it agent-friendly, making it robust, and Astra did a lot of it for me, very reliably.

In Codex it's more by-the-book overall and not great at creative ideas; it's more point-and-shoot, faithful and dependable. Put it on PR review and I'll trust it unconditionally; it always spots the engineering problems.

### Opus 5.5

I've only used it for two days, but in those two days I've felt I could do anything with it. It gets what I mean very quickly, and it's good at suggestions that make me sit up. It's smarter than any other model.

Together we've tried more ambitious, much bolder things. Using Opus is a pleasure for me; it feels very free.

It isn't actually that reliable at writing code; on some details of execution it's behind Astra. But on architecture, on deciding what to build, it's in a class of its own.

Since it came out I've barely opened Codex. It's just that good: smart, understanding, creative, and bold.

## Chinese models

I try pretty much every Chinese model and harness, but because they just aren't as far ahead, and cost more, I use them very little.

### DeepSeek

DeepSeek has never done much for me. Maybe that's because I keep comparing it with Astra, Fable or Sol, which isn't fair on it.

### Kimi K3

When it first came out I was in Qingdao. I tried it that night, thought its front-end taste was really good, and bought a subscription on the spot. (Though looking back now, Kimi might have been routing me to Fable at the time, who knows?)

### GLM-5.3

I've worked with it a little. On small, contained coding tasks its quality is decent. Of course I never gave it big jobs; the biggest job I gave it was a bit smaller than the smallest I gave Sol.

### Qwen 3.8

A fairly good impression. It pointed out front-end problems straight away (though that was exactly what I asked it for), which led to some of the changes we made later.

### MiMo 2.6 Pro

~~I've never had a good impression of Xiaomi's models.~~

AIOJ was hooked up to MiMo from the start, and it was really hard to use; we even built some workflows and adaptations just for it.

But after joining the MiMo Desktop beta in September and trying MiMo 2.6 Pro, I feel it may be the Chinese model closest to Opus 5.5 in temperament, though a tier weaker overall. A lot of her "complaints" (that's basically the only job I give her) are really constructive. Sure, more of them were checked by Astra and turned out not to be real, but she comes up with plenty of good suggestions.

She's a bit scatterbrained when coding, and in that I feel she's like Opus too: they both look more broadly and think more freely. The GPT models are all more focused.
