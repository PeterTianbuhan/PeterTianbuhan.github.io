### Attention wasn't invented by Google

7 November 2026

In 2014, Dzmitry Bahdanau was a 23-year-old master's student in Bengio's lab at the University of Montreal, doing research for the first time. His job was to get a machine to translate English into French. The models back then had a problem: they had to squeeze a whole sentence into one vector before translating it, and long sentences got forgotten in the squeeze.

Bahdanau remembered doing translation exercises as a kid, his eyes moving back and forth between the original and the translation. He made the model do the same: for each word it translated, it looked back at the original and picked out the words that mattered at that moment. That mechanism later became known as attention — the word "attention" was added later by his supervisor Bengio; Bahdanau himself called the model RNNsearch. It worked on the first try.

Three years later, Google's Transformer threw out everything in the model except attention, and that gave us today's large models.
